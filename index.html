01 The Problem

Software is rarely expensive to build. It is expensive to change.

Every system carries the cost of its own decisions. Made early, a correction is nearly free. Made late — after launch, after your team has built on top of it — the same correction costs orders of magnitude more.

This is not a metaphor. It is the single most reliable pattern in software delivery, and it is the reason most "successful" launches quietly become unfixable within eighteen months.

In discovery 1× Redraw the architecture on a whiteboard. Cost: a conversation.
During build 10× Refactor the module, migrate the data, re-test the surface.
In production 100× Coordinate a migration, absorb downtime, apologise to customers, rebuild trust.

We exist to make the expensive decisions while they are still cheap to make — and to build the kind of foundation that does not force you back here.

02 What We Are

An engineering partner, not a vendor.

The name is the model, literally: a dev centre that holds every discipline under one roof, and a single point of contact you can hold accountable.

DEV-CENTER-POINT

A vendor takes your specification, builds exactly what it says, and is finished the moment it compiles. That is a transaction, and it is why so much software arrives complete and unusable.

We work the other way around. We interrogate the problem until the right scope is obvious, then commit to it in writing. The person who scopes your project is the person who architects it.

What we refuse Fixed-bid fiction, unpaid "test projects", and code we would not be willing to maintain ourselves.

04 How We Work

Five non-negotiables, held on every project.

These are not values on a wall. They are operating rules, and they are the first things we are willing to be judged on.

M—01

Written before spoken

Every architectural decision is recorded in a short document — including the options we rejected and why. Six months later, nobody has to guess what we were thinking.

M—02

Boring where it counts

We spend novelty on your product, never on your infrastructure. Proven tools in the critical path; anything new has to earn its place with a reason you can read.

M—03

Observable from day one

Logs, traces and alerts ship with the first feature, not the first incident. You should never be the one to discover that your system is down.

M—04

Reversible by default

Every change can be undone in a single step. The rollback path is written before the migration is, and tested before it is needed.

M—05

Your team can run it

We optimise for the day we are no longer in the room. Documentation, pairing and a real handover are part of the build — never an afterthought bolted on at the end.

06 Who Stands Behind It

There is no “I” in the code we hand you.

Teams fail software far more often than code does. So we removed the gaps where that usually happens: no sales layer between you and the engineer, no architect who never sees production, no ticket queue that swallows context.

The people who scope your project are the people who build it — and the people who answer when it breaks. A system is only ever as dependable as the specific humans who promised it, so we make ours accountable by name.
